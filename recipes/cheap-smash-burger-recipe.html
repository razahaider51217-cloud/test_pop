Cheap Smash Burger Recipe (Under $3 a Serving)

A smash burger is the cheapest way to eat like you are at a restaurant. You need very little beef, a screaming hot pan and a hard press - the crust does the rest. This is the exact method our line cooks use at Sarab, scaled down for a home kitchen and priced at $2.88 a serving.

Why This Recipe Is So Cheap

  • Ground beef is the cheapest protein per gram - you only need 4 oz per burger.
  • No special equipment: one heavy frying pan and a stiff spatula are enough.
  • Bread, onions and pickles cost pennies and stretch the meal to four servings.

Ingredients (makes 4 servings)

Prices are typical New York supermarket prices in September 2026 and come to about $11.50 in total - that is $2.88 per serving.

  • 1 lb (450 g) ground beef, 80/20 blend - $5.49
  • 4 brioche or sesame buns - $3.29
  • 4 slices American or cheddar cheese - $0.79
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced - $0.60
  • 3 tbsp pickles - $0.45
  • 3 tbsp mayonnaise, 1 tbsp ketchup, 1 tsp mustard (special sauce) - $0.38
  • 1 tbsp butter, softened - $0.20
  • Kosher salt and coarse black pepper - $0.30
  • Optional: lettuce, tomato or jalapenos from the fridge

How to Make It, Step by Step

  1. Divide the beef into 4 loose balls of about 4 oz each. Do not compact them - loose piles smash better and stay juicy.
  2. Mix the mayonnaise, ketchup and mustard in a small bowl and set the special sauce aside. Split and butter the buns.
  3. Heat a heavy frying pan or flat griddle over high heat for 3 minutes until a drop of water sizzles instantly.
  4. Place one beef ball in the pan and immediately press it flat with a stiff spatula for 10 seconds. Season with salt and pepper.
  5. Cook for 90 seconds until the edges are dark and lacy, then scrape underneath, flip once and add a cheese slice. Cook 30 seconds more.
  6. Toast the buttered buns cut-side down in the same pan for 45 seconds, then build: sauce, pickles, onion, patty, lettuce.
  7. Rest the burgers for 1 minute before serving so the juices settle back into the meat.

Chef Tips That Keep the Cost Down

  • Press only once. Pressing again squeezes out the juice that makes a smash burger taste expensive.
  • Do not salt the beef before it hits the pan - early salt makes the patty dense and rubbery.
  • No cast iron? Use the heaviest pan you own and never crowd it - two patties at a time maximum.
  • Scale up: the same mix makes 12 sliders for about $26, ideal for a cheap game-night spread.
